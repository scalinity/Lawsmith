## The registry and the AC7 boundary

A registry entry holds what belongs to its kind and nothing else:

| Primitive entry | Region entry |
| --- | --- |
| kind, capability, title, shelf verb, glyph | kind, capability, title |
| file keys (`direction: vec3`, `strength: number`, …) | file keys (`halfExtents`, `radius`, `halfHeight`) |
| `validate` (bounds, direction normalization) | `validate` (bounds) |
| `compile` → local evaluator `(r, out) → K` writing local `A` | `compile` → gauge `r → d` |
| scalar controls: label, unit, bounds, step, undo label | scalar controls |
| handle rails (strength, core) | handle rails (extents), fade corner, bounding half-extents, narrowest dimension, ray entry, conversion from bounds |
| defaults, default region, created color, summary, description | |

The generic paths name no kind. Host stepping and its single force path (the kernel's `(A, K)` and the aggregate adapter), command scheduling, validation, the file reader and writer, the capability list, recovery and the Save/Open workflows consume registry entries. There is no primitive-specific special case in simulation stepping or persistence orchestration. Type-specific presentation and interaction logic stays in the modules it belongs to: `worldView.ts` keeps unit render geometry per region kind and branches on the registry's glyph (dots and a gauge for drag, an axis line for a vortex), and handle rails, creation defaults, inspector controls and summaries come from each entry. Tests and examples are per kind, and M1's box-only fixture script `scriptedRecipeEdits` remains for the M1 suites. Adding a fifth primitive means its registry entry with its capability, its tests and an example. A new glyph or region kind also needs its presentation in `worldView.ts`.

Schema 1 is unchanged: the M2 reader already reserved these capability names and rejected them as unsupported. They are now `region.sphere.v1`, `region.cylinderY.v1`, `primitive.softRadial.v1`, `primitive.vortexY.v1`, `primitive.linearDrag.v1`, beside M2's three. A build without them rejects a scene that declares them. Operator kinds (`sum`, `gain`, `mask`) are still rejected, naming their capabilities.

## The aggregate adapter (T03)

`a* = β(Kh)·(A − K·v)` with `A = g + ΣA_i`, `K = ΣK_i`, `β = −expm1(−Kh)/(Kh)` (1 at Kh = 0), then `λ = min(1, maxApplied/|a*|)`, then `F = mass·λ·a*` once. `tests/drag.test.ts`; the closed-form references use `Math.exp`, never the adapter.

| --- | --- | --- |
| β at Kh = 0 | exactly 1 | pass |
| β for z = 5e-324 … 1e-3 | within 2e-16 of `1 − z/2 + z²/6 − z³/24 + z⁴/120`; no cut-off | pass |
| Why expm1 | the naive `(1 − e^{−z})/z` is off by more than 1e-8 at z = 1e-11; β by at most 2e-16 | pass |
| β·z = 1 − e^{−z}, z up to 100; β decreasing | 1e-15 | pass |

## Spatial handles (T05)

Adjust mode (S) shows the selected law's handles; Move (T) and Rotate (R) keep TransformControls. Each handle slides on a rail in the law's frame and sets one control. A grab offset keeps a press from jumping the value. Values hold at their bounds while the pointer goes beyond, so the saved value is the one shown.

| Handle | Rail | Sets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Box faces | −X, +Y, −Z face centers | one half-extent each |
| Sphere | top | the one radius |
| Y-cylinder | −X and top | radius (X and Z together) and half-height |
| Fade (every region) | center → outer corner; sits on the inner surface d = 1 − f | f only |
| Directional strength | arrow tip from the center along the direction, 0.05 m per m/s² (the drive arrows' scale) | strength 0–200 |
| Radial and vortex strength | arrow at an anchor halfway out on +X, along the true drive there (inward; −Z); dragging through the anchor reverses the sign | strength −200–200 |
| Core radius | along +Z, with a ring of radius ε | ε 0.01–100 |
| Drag coefficient | a gauge without an arrowhead, 0.2 m per s⁻¹ | coefficient 0–100 |

Vitest (`tests/authoring.test.ts`): every handle of every kind reproduces its law at its own rail position; the fade handle lies on the inner surface for every region; extent handles change only their dimension and never strength, core or fade; a resize leaves the law's value at a fixed interior point identical; rails follow the pose into world space; a view ray picks the closest rail point. **Why a body's motion can differ from the law's arrow.** The arrows draw the external drive A the kernel computes at sample points. A body in the clump also receives contact impulses from its neighbors, which Rapier's solver computes and the arrows do not include, so its velocity change is the law's contribution plus contacts. The collision fixtures show the same thing numerically. M4 owns the per-body explanation.

**Cylinder and axis.** The vortex's local +Y axis is drawn through its support when selected. ## Findings

1. **Pure drag in the f32 engine, past 19 overlapping maximum laws.** The adapter's f64 output never reverses a velocity for any supported aggregate K (up to 32 × 100 s⁻¹). Rapier integrates `v + dt·F/m` in f32, so once `e^{−Kh}` falls below f32's precision (Kh ≳ 16, K ≳ 1900 s⁻¹, which takes about 19 fully overlapping 100 s⁻¹ laws) the engine's rounding can leave a residual about one f32 ulp of the previous speed (≈ 7e-9 m/s from 0.5 m/s) on the far side of zero. Speed never grows. Using the engine's f32 `dt` in β would not remove it, because the rounding is inside Rapier's step. The fixture pins the bound. **Owner decision:** accept it as the engine's precision floor, or bound the aggregate drag a scene may stack (a SPEC change).
2. **`editsSinceReset` in `run-digest` undercounts.** An edit whose acknowledgment is adopted inside the document controller's `settle()` (a toggle followed by its digest report, for example) is not counted, so a digest can say `editsSinceReset: 0` after an edit (Overlap's tick-1200 digest did). Diagnostic only, pre-existing since M1; the host's `lastAppliedSequence` is the exact count. Low.
3. **Synthetic drags reach WebKit only in part** (above). Informational; M2 finding 3.
4. **Native QA shares the screen.** Another session's app, its crash dialog and notification banners took focus or covered the window during this unit's runs. The guards refused every such click and keystroke, and the affected runs were repeated. The harness now waits out a notification banner (up to 10 s) and skips exactly the recording overlay of its own `screencapture`. Informational.
5. **ffmpeg's avfoundation screen input delivers no frames on macOS 27.2**, so recordings use `screencapture -v` for a fixed length, transcoded to H.264. Informational.
